# Design Directions

## Purpose

Develop three distinct, coherent, and equally viable design directions
within the design space established by the canonical inputs and the
Design Boundaries phase.

Create the directions before expanding them into detailed website plans
or design specifications.

## Direction Development

Define one coherent design concept for each proposal.

Each direction should establish a clear design character that can guide
later decisions about information hierarchy, structure, visual language,
spacing, imagery, UI patterns, interaction, and responsive composition.

Do not finalize detailed pages, routes, design tokens, or implementation
details in this phase.

## Meaningful Differentiation

Differentiate the proposals across several relevant design dimensions
where the permitted design space allows.

Relevant dimensions may include:

- information hierarchy
- structural tendency
- navigation emphasis
- section composition
- typography character
- visual density
- spacing
- imagery treatment
- geometric language
- UI pattern character
- motion character
- responsive composition

Do not rely on isolated cosmetic changes as the primary source of differentiation.

## Scope Preservation

All directions must remain based on the same canonical customer information
and website requirements.

Do not create differentiation by removing mandatory requirements,
inventing customer facts, or adding unsupported functional scope.

Optional customer requirements may be treated differently where their
supplied strength permits.

## Constraint Preservation

Fixed and constrained decisions take precedence over proposal diversity.

Differentiate only within the remaining permitted design space.

When constraints leave little freedom, prefer meaningful but limited
variation over violating customer intent for the sake of novelty.

## Equal Viability

Treat all three directions as serious customer-facing alternatives.

Do not create filler, intentionally weak, throwaway, or deliberately
experimental proposals merely to satisfy the proposal count.

Do not rank or recommend one direction over the others.

## Direction Identity

Give each direction a concise, descriptive name and concept.

Names should communicate meaningful design character rather than generic
quality claims.

Concept descriptions must describe the design direction without presenting
unsupported customer, audience, market, or performance claims as fact.

## Coherence

Treat each direction as an integrated design idea.

Later decisions should follow from the direction rather than being assembled
from unrelated stylistic choices.

Use the direction to maintain coherence across structure, hierarchy,
visual language, interaction, and responsive behavior.

## Preliminary Structural Intent

A direction may establish a high-level structural tendency when useful,
such as single-page, multi-page, or hybrid emphasis.

Do not finalize pages, routes, navigation targets, or section trees in this phase.

## Preliminary Visual Intent

A direction may establish qualitative visual intent such as typography
character, palette character, density, imagery style, or motion character.

Do not finalize concrete color tokens, typography scales, spacing values,
or implementation-level styling here.

## Differentiation Check

Before continuing, compare the three directions together.

Confirm that:

- each is internally coherent;
- each is viable within the same requirement scope;
- none is intentionally weaker than the others;
- differences extend beyond isolated cosmetic changes;
- no direction depends on invented facts or unsupported features;
- fixed and constrained decisions remain preserved.

## Handoff

After establishing three viable and meaningfully distinct directions,
continue to Information Architecture Planning.

Develop the structure of each proposal according to its direction while
preserving the same canonical requirement basis.
