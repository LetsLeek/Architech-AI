# Proposal Differentiation

## Purpose

Review the three fully developed design proposals together and confirm that
they remain meaningfully distinct, coherent, equally viable customer-facing
alternatives within the permitted design space.

Differentiation must not change the underlying mandatory customer scope or
violate authoritative constraints.

## Review Timing

Perform this review after each proposal has been developed through information
architecture, pages, navigation, sections, elements, design specification,
responsive behavior, localization where applicable, and traceability.

Do not rely only on the early design-direction labels.

Evaluate the actual completed proposal structures and design systems.

## Meaningful Difference

Ensure the proposals differ in multiple relevant design dimensions where
the permitted design space allows.

Relevant dimensions may include:

- information architecture;
- page structure;
- content emphasis;
- navigation;
- section composition;
- visual hierarchy;
- typography character;
- palette usage;
- spacing and density;
- imagery treatment;
- UI pattern language;
- motion character;
- responsive composition.

Do not require every dimension to differ.

## Perceptible Difference

Differences should be meaningful enough to be visible or experientially
recognizable in the resulting customer previews.

Do not treat trivial token changes, minor spacing differences, naming changes,
or isolated cosmetic variations as sufficient differentiation by themselves.

## Requirement Preservation

Preserve the same mandatory customer requirement basis across all proposals.

Do not create diversity by omitting mandatory content, functionality,
languages, or constraints from one proposal.

Optional could requirements may vary where their supplied strength permits.

Should requirements should not be intentionally weakened merely to create contrast.

## Scope Preservation

Differentiate the design solution, not the underlying product scope.

Do not add unsupported features, integrations, capabilities, customer facts,
or functional requirements merely to make one proposal appear different.

## Constraint Preservation

Authoritative customer and workflow constraints take precedence over diversity.

When constraints limit the design space, differentiate only within the remaining freedom.

Prefer honest similarity over violating constraints for the sake of novelty.

## Equal Viability

Treat all three proposals as serious, complete, implementation-ready options.

Do not produce filler, intentionally weak, incomplete, generic, or deliberately
inferior proposals.

Do not designate a winner, recommended proposal, default proposal, or ranking.

## Coherence

Ensure each proposal remains internally coherent.

A proposal must not become a random collection of stylistic differences merely
to distinguish it from the others.

Its pages, hierarchy, visual system, imagery, interaction, and responsive behavior
should still express one integrated design direction.

## Differentiation Review

Compare the proposals side by side across the relevant design dimensions.

Use an internal comparison model when helpful.

Do not emit a separate differentiation matrix or scoring artifact.

If two proposals have converged too closely during detailed planning, refine the
current candidate designs within the same Designer Agent execution before final output.

Do not initiate additional AgentExecutions or workflow retries.

## Evidence Boundary

Do not justify proposal differences using unsupported customer preferences,
market assumptions, conversion claims, audience behavior, or performance claims.

Describe design differences as design decisions unless authoritative input
supports a stronger statement.

## Naming Consistency

Ensure each proposal name and concept still accurately describe the fully
developed proposal after detailed planning and refinement.

Do not use different names to disguise materially similar proposals.

## Final Differentiation Check

Before continuing, confirm that:

- all three proposals are complete and implementation-ready;
- all mandatory requirements are preserved;
- all authoritative constraints are preserved;
- each proposal has a coherent independent design identity;
- differences are perceptible beyond isolated cosmetic changes where freedom permits;
- no proposal is intentionally weaker than another;
- no unsupported feature scope or customer facts were introduced;
- names and concepts match the actual completed designs;
- no proposal is ranked or recommended.

## Handoff

After confirming meaningful differentiation, continue to Cross-Proposal Consistency Review.

Verify that the three alternatives remain aligned to the same canonical inputs,
reference domains, mandatory scope, and output contract before finalization.
